# Roadmap

The route from card 1 to B1: eighty-five blocks of about forty cards, each with
the grammar it installs and the vocabulary it spends. Written order is fixed —
a block may only use grammar and words from blocks before it.

The grammar finishes at card 1100. Everything after that is vocabulary on frames
already owned, because that is where the gap actually is: grammar is a few dozen
facts and B1 comprehension is a couple of thousand words.

This is a plan, not the deck. The deck is [sentences.md](sentences.md), and
every word is re-checked against [vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md) at writing time.

**Status:** blocks 1–10 written (375 cards) · block 11 next.

From block 6 on, every block also carries a **Revives** line: the elements it
owes an appearance under [rules.md](rules.md) rule 4. Run `npm run reuse` to see
the current ledger — it is the source, this is the plan.

## Part I — The essential verbs (blocks 1–7, cards 1–271)

One verb per block, each the same shape — see "How a verb enters" in
[rules.md](rules.md). Block 3 is the hinge: after `Je veux + infinitive` exists,
every remaining verb enters at one card each.

### Block 1 — être · cards 1–43 ✅

**Grammar** être (all six forms) · the nine subject pronouns · adjective
agreement (free) · `ne … pas` · elision `n'` / `qu'` (free) · `est-ce que` ·
intonation questions · `où` · `Où est … ?` · `c'est` / `ce n'est pas` · `ça`

**Vocabulary** fatigué · occupé · malade · content · ici · là · ensemble ·
en retard · aussi · très · bien · mais · le bus · l'ami · ma/mon · la voiture

### Block 2 — avoir · cards 44–76 ✅

**Grammar** avoir (all six forms) · `un / une / des` · `de` replacing them under
negation · `le` surviving negation · `il y a` / `il n'y a pas`

**Vocabulary** deux · avoir faim · avoir soif · le temps · avoir besoin de ·
le problème

### Block 3 — vouloir · cards 77–115 ✅

The hinge. Opens the infinitive slot.

**Grammar** vouloir (all six forms) · **infinitive after a conjugated verb** ·
`du / de la / de l'` (partitive) · negation around a verb + infinitive

**Vocabulary** le café · l'eau · le pain · manger · boire · partir · travailler ·
aider · quelque chose · maintenant · un peu

### Block 4 — pouvoir · cards 116–150 ✅

**Grammar** pouvoir (all six forms) · `ne … pas` around a verb + infinitive ·
asking permission at three levels of register (`Est-ce que je peux …?`,
`Je peux …?`, `Pouvez-vous …?`) · `pour` + infinitive · pouvoir against vouloir

**Vocabulary** entrer · sortir · rester · parler · regarder · pour · libre ·
merci · s'il vous plaît · oui · non · d'accord · peut-être

### Block 5 — devoir · cards 151–185 ✅

**Grammar** devoir (all six forms) · obligation against wanting against being
able, drilled directly · `il faut` (frozen, impersonal) · `il ne faut pas` as a
prohibition rather than a release · `pourquoi` / `parce que` · `je voudrais` as
a frozen politeness form

**Vocabulary** payer · appeler · demander · trouver · chercher · l'argent ·
important · tôt · tard · vite

`pourquoi` is pulled forward from block 27; `le travail` and `la maison` are
pushed back to block 6.

### Block 6 — aller · cards 186–231 ✅

**Grammar** aller (all six forms) · `à` + place, `au / à la / à l'` ·
**aller + infinitive** (near future) · `ça va` (frozen — the verb underneath is
now known) · `Comment allez-vous ?` (inversion formula)

**Vocabulary** le travail · la maison · le marché · le bureau · la ville ·
le restaurant · la gare · l'école · demain · aujourd'hui · comment · mal

**Revives** `où` (silent since c38 — and the natural question for a motion verb:
*Où est-ce que tu vas ?*) · `aussi` · `des` · a partitive · `de` under negation ·
`et` · `ou` · plural forms of two earlier verbs (`avons` / `avez` / `ont` are the
stalest) · `vous êtes`, dead since c13

### Block 7 — faire · cards 232–271 ✅

**Grammar** faire (all six forms — five spellings, since `fais` serves je and
tu), glossed **to do / to make** from card one · `faire` + activity, and the
article each activity insists on (`du sport`, `la cuisine`, `le ménage`) ·
impersonal weather `il fait` · `qu'est-ce que` / `quoi` · `Quel temps fait-il ?`
(frozen)

**Vocabulary** le sport · la cuisine · le ménage · chaud · froid · beau ·
le temps (weather, inside the frozen question) · quoi

**Revives** `aussi` (silent since c16) · `soif` and `avez` (since block 2) ·
`voulons` · `veulent` · `pouvons` · `occupé` · `le temps` under negation ·
`ensemble` · `avec` · `sortir` · `de l'eau` · `des` · a partitive · `de` under
negation · `et` · `ou` · `mais`

`qu'est-ce que` and `quoi` are pulled forward from block 24; `quel` comes with
them only inside `Quel temps fait-il ?`, as a fixed question rather than as the
agreeing determiner, which stays in block 24. `le ménage` was missing from
[vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md) and was added.

## Part II — Filling out the present (blocks 8–14, cards 272–510)

The essential verbs are done. Regular patterns install once, then new verbs are
nearly free.

### Block 8 — regular *-er* verbs · cards 272–310 ✅

**Grammar** the *-er* endings, all six persons, installed on `parler` — which
arrives as a bare infinitive in block 4 · the silent `-ent`, and `il parle` /
`ils parlent` being one sound end to end · then `regarder` at one element with
its forms free, and every regular *-er* verb after it the same
([rules.md](rules.md), "The *-er* endings")

**Vocabulary** donner · habiter · aimer · penser · écouter · la télévision ·
la musique · la personne

**Revives** `vite` · `deux` (silent since c60) · `un peu` · `avec` · `argent` ·
`pourquoi` · `ensemble` · `avez` · `pouvons`/`pouvez` · `des` · a partitive ·
`de` under negation · `et` · `ou` · `mais`

`montrer` is pushed to block 9: it wants an indirect object, and the pronouns
for that arrive later. `la personne` was missing from
[vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md) and was added — note the collision with
`personne` meaning *nobody*, which arrives with `ne … personne` in block 24.

### Block 9 — more *-er*, adverbs, frequency · cards 311–350 ✅

**Grammar** one card switching the *-er* endings on for every infinitive
already taught (`travailler`, `aider`, `chercher`, `trouver`, `rester`,
`entrer`) · *-er* verbs at one card each · adverb placement, after the verb ·
frequency · `ne … jamais`, and the article it flattens

**Vocabulary** arriver · commencer (+ `commençons`) · arrêter · oublier ·
toujours · souvent · beaucoup · assez · déjà

**Revives** `oui` · `d'accord` · `peut-être` · `entrer` · `as` · `sommes` ·
`veut` · `peuvent` · `faim` · `en retard` · `des` · a partitive · `de` under
negation · `et` · `ou` · `mais`

`essayer` (*j'essaie*) and `préférer` (*je préfère*) move out: both change their
stem, so under [rules.md](rules.md) they are not free. They belong with `manger`,
`payer` and `appeler` — three infinitives the deck already owes forms for — in a
stem-changer block. `montrer` is dropped from the plan entirely; it wants an
indirect object and the pronouns for that arrive far later.

### Block 10 — stem-changing *-er* verbs · cards 351–375 ✅

**Grammar** the stem moves in the four forms where the ending is silent (je, tu,
il, ils) and holds at nous and vous · `manger` and `commencer` as the opposite
case, changing only at nous and only for spelling · `payer` = to pay *for*, no
preposition

**Vocabulary** essayer · préférer · acheter · plus the forms of `manger`,
`payer` and `appeler`, which have been unusable infinitives since blocks 3–5

**Revives** `devez` · `ils ont` · `la personne` · `le bus` · `arriver` ·
`aussi` · `des` · a partitive · `de` under negation · `et` · `ou` · `mais`

First block written under the subject rule: a third of its cards open on
something that is not a person, four of them on a plain noun.

Inserted ahead of the *-ir* verbs: block 8 made the regular endings free, so
leaving five exceptions unplaced would have had the learner producing
*nous mangons* and *j'appele*.

### Block 11 — *-ir* verbs · cards 376–410

**Grammar** the *finir* endings · the *partir / sortir / dormir* subgroup and why
it differs

**Vocabulary** finir · choisir · réussir · dormir · servir · la nuit ·
le matin · le soir

### Block 12 — *-re* verbs and *prendre* · cards 411–450

**Grammar** the *vendre* endings · `prendre` irregular, with `prendre un café` as
its own idiomatic card · `comprendre` / `apprendre` inherit it

**Vocabulary** vendre · répondre · entendre · prendre · comprendre · apprendre ·
le train · le métro · le livre

### Block 13 — the high-frequency irregulars · cards 451–495

**Grammar** `venir` · `voir` · `savoir` · `connaître` · `dire` · `mettre`, six
forms each · **savoir vs. connaître** on its own cards

**Vocabulary** those six verbs · la vérité · le nom · la chose

### Block 14 — reflexive verbs · cards 496–535

**Grammar** `se` + verb · the reflexive pronouns across all persons · position
under negation and after a modal · daily routine as the frame

**Vocabulary** se lever · se coucher · se laver · s'habiller · se dépêcher ·
se reposer · se rappeler

## Part VII — Closing the grammar (block 29, cards 1061–1100)

The structures blocks 16–27 stepped around. They are last because each one is a
small correction to something already fluent, not a new system — and because
none of them blocks comprehension until the rest is solid.

### Block 29 — the remainder · cards 1086–1125

**Grammar** the present subjunctive after `il faut que` / `vouloir que` /
`avant que`, on the six verbs whose subjunctive is worth knowing (`être`,

`en` + present participle (`en faisant`) · `depuis` + present, against
`il y a` + past · two pronouns in one sentence (`je le lui donne`) · `on` doing
the work English gives the passive

**Vocabulary** none — the block is grammar on words already owned.

## Part VIII — The vocabulary grind (blocks 30–85, cards 1101–3340)

Grammar is done. From here the deck has one job: put the remaining **1,880
words** of [vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md) into sentences.

Nothing about rule 1 changes — still one new thing per card. What changes is the
*hit rate*. Blocks 1–27 spend about **0.4 new words per card**, because half
their cards are paradigm drills and rest cards holding a frame still while a form
lands. With no new grammar to install, the frame no longer has to be held: rest
cards fall to roughly one in seven, and the deck runs at about **0.85 new words
per card**. That is the whole reason this part is affordable — 1,880 words at
the Part I rate would be 4,700 cards, and at this rate it is 2,240.

### How a vocabulary block is shaped

The verb-block shape (object on the table → nine forms → spend it) has nothing
left to teach and does not apply. In its place:

1. **Rule 3 flips — vary the frame instead of holding it still.** The learner
   owns every structure in the deck, so a block that reuses one frame is wasting
   the only thing it has left to practise. Two consecutive cards should not
   share a shape.
2. **One bucket never arrives in one lump.** Each block draws from **two or
   three** buckets below, so a theme is met, dropped, and met again forty cards
   later rather than exhausted in one sitting.
3. **Grammar rotation.** Grammar now decays instead of arriving, and the SRS
   cannot help — it schedules cards, not structures. So every block spends its
   sentences across: one past tense · one future or conditional · one object
   pronoun · one relative clause (`qui` / `que`) · one negation beyond
   `ne … pas`. Five cards out of forty, and the whole grammar stays alive.
4. **The reuse quotas of rule 4 stand unchanged**, and matter more here than
   anywhere: this part is where a word taught once and never revisited is most
   likely to die.

### Tier B — the rest · blocks 67–85 · cards 2581–3340

The ~640 words that specialise: emotions & personality (65) · professions (50) ·
health & medical (45) · sports & leisure (45) · law, government & society (41) ·
kitchen & cooking (38) · transportation (36) · school & education (36) ·
work & office (33) · technology (31) · communication (31) · money & shopping
(27) · materials (11) · **abstract nouns (155)**.

The abstract nouns are last on purpose. They are the hardest to put in a
sentence a person would actually say, and the least use in comprehension —
but they are also where B1 turns into B2, so they get real blocks rather than
being sprinkled.

### What the milestones actually are

| card | words known | what that buys |
| ---- | ----------- | -------------- |
| 1100 | ~550 | every structure in B1. Vocabulary is the only thing missing from here on. |
| ~2100 | ~1,400 | simple kids' shows followed with gaps — the plot, not every line. |
| 2580 | ~1,750 | kids' media comfortably. B1 comprehension. |
| 3340 | ~2,100 | all of vocabulary.md. Solid B1, reaching into B2 vocabulary. |

Beyond card 3340 there is no plan and does not need to be one: the dump is
spent, and what follows is whatever the learner reads and hears, mined for the
words it turns out they are missing.
