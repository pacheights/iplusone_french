# Spine plan — the whole language, to a point

The full **Introduction Sequence** the generator walks down. This is the map; the
item-level format is shown in [sequence-proof.md](sequence-proof.md) (Stage A, items
1–30). Governed by [curriculum.md](curriculum.md); vocabulary drawn from
[vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md).

**Ceiling — "to a point": everyday fluency.** Present, passé composé, imparfait, futur
proche + futur simple, conditional, imperative; object + relative pronouns; comparatives;
the common connectors and prepositions. **Stops before** the subjunctive, passé simple,
and literary tenses. This is roughly an A2→B1 course — enough to handle daily life,
past and future, without the abstract endgame.

**Size.** ~1,500 distinct spine items (≈ the dedupable lemmas in vocabulary.md) plus the
conjugation cards the policy below multiplies out — call it **~1,500–1,800 cards total**.
Not one flat list: the sequencing *care* is front-loaded, the *volume* is back-loaded.

## Conjugation schedule (the size lever)

- **Anchors** — être, avoir, aller, faire: full person paradigm, and re-drilled in full
  each time a new tense is introduced.
- **Every irregular verb** (prendre, venir, voir, dire, mettre, savoir, vouloir, pouvoir,
  devoir, boire, lire, écrire, partir, sortir, dormir, connaître, …): full present
  paradigm; the frequent ones also get full passé composé / imparfait / futur.
- **Regular verbs** — full paradigm on the **first few of each group** (-er, -ir, -re) to
  set the pattern; after that each new regular enters as **infinitive + one or two forms**,
  then **infinitive-only** once the pattern is implicit ("trail off").
- **Reflexives** (se laver, se lever, se coucher…) get their own paradigm treatment once —
  the pronoun-agreement pattern is the new thing, reused thereafter.

## Density is not uniform

| | grammar per item | sequencing care | off-the-rails risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stages A–C** (the scaffold) | high | hand-ordered, card by card | **high** — all of it lives here |
| **Stage D** (the tail) | ~none | ranked + bucketed, not hand-reasoned | low — stable frames |

The generator only wanders while grammar is still being introduced. Once the scaffold is
complete (end of Stage C), adding the 900th noun is pure vocabulary on frames the learner
already owns — safe to generate in volume.

## Stage A — Present-tense core (~items 1–140)

**Grammar:** subject pronouns (je…elles, on); le/la/les, un/une/des, partitive du/de la,
au/aux; **être, avoir, aller, faire** full present; first regular -er verbs full present;
modals **vouloir, pouvoir, devoir** + infinitive; **ne…pas**; questions (intonation,
est-ce que, qu'est-ce que, où, quand, comment, pourquoi, combien, qui); et/ou/mais/donc;
parce que, quand, si; core prepositions (à, de, dans, avec, pour, sur, sous, chez, sans);
adjective agreement; possessives (mon/ton/son…); demonstratives (ce/cette/ces).
**Vocabulary:** highest-frequency nouns/adjectives, rotated across drink, work, people,
home, time, feelings. **Endpoint:** the learner makes simple true statements, negations,
and questions about the present.

## Stage B — Filling out the present + first past & future (~items 140–380)

**Grammar:** regular -ir/-re verbs (finir, choisir, vendre, attendre); the irregular verb
set (prendre, venir, voir, dire, mettre, savoir, connaître, partir, sortir, dormir, boire,
lire, écrire) full present; **reflexive verbs**; **futur proche** (aller + inf); **passé
composé** with avoir, then with être (+ agreement); object pronouns (le/la/les, lui/leur,
me/te/nous/vous, y, en); comparatives/superlatives; adverbs of frequency, manner, time.
**Vocabulary:** numbers, time/calendar, family, body, food, house, city, transport.
**Endpoint:** the learner narrates what happened today and what's about to happen.

## Stage C — Imparfait, futur simple, conditional (~items 380–620)

**Grammar:** **imparfait** and the **passé composé vs. imparfait** contrast (the central
B1 hurdle — habitual/background vs. completed event); **futur simple**; **conditionnel**
(politeness "je voudrais", then *si* + imparfait → conditional hypotheticals); **relative
pronouns** qui/que/où/dont; **imperative** (incl. with pronouns); richer subordinators
(pendant que, avant de, après, puisque, comme, alors que); negation variants
(ne…plus/jamais/rien/personne). **Vocabulary:** professions, health, money, emotions,
nature, abstract nouns. **Endpoint:** the learner tells a past story and talks about
hypotheticals and plans.

## Stage D — The content tail (~items 620–1,500)

**Grammar:** essentially complete. Mop-up only where high-value: passive, gerund
(en + -ant), depuis / il y a with time expressions, irregular comparatives (meilleur,
mieux). **Vocabulary:** the long tail — the remaining domains of vocabulary.md,
frequency-ranked and **rotated across domains** so no theme is taught in a block. New
verbs here enter mostly as **infinitive + passé composé**, relying on transfer. This is
the bulk of the item count and the least hand-work per item.

---

## Build order

1. **Stage A in full** (~140 items) — the highest-care, highest-value band; the part that
   most needs a human eye before generation. Next deliverable.
2. Stages B and C — hand-ordered like A, one band at a time so each stays reviewable.
3. Stage D — assembled as ranked domain buckets, then generated in volume against the
   now-stable frames.

Sentences get rendered against the spine one band at a time, never all at once — that
keeps each batch reviewable and keeps the generator inside frames it already knows.
